# Assignment 1: Data Reporting Critique

Story: [Airbnb Snowfalls in Vermont Ski Towns, Bringing Cash and Concerns] (https://www.sevendaysvt.com/news/airbnb-snowballs-in-vermont-ski-towns-bringing-cash-and-concerns-10220050/) by Alicia Freese

## Clarity and Accuracy
Central Insight - 5/5 
The central insight is that Airbnb has become a significant aspect of the housing and tourism economies in Vermont ski towns, creating financial opportunities and concerns about housing availability. The story does a nice job of showing that short-term rentals can benefit individual homeowners and local economies while potentially making it more difficult for residents and workers to find affordable housing. The headline, "Airbnb Snowfalls in Vermont Ski Towns, Bringing Cash and Concerns," effectively communicates this central idea. The words "cash and concerns" immediately cement the two sides of the issue that the story explores.

## Data Visualization - 4/5
The story includes an interactive visualization showing data about the five Vermont communities with the most Airbnb listings. This visualization gives readers a way to look at the data in depth instead of only reading statistics in the story. The graphic is relatively easy to understand, but it could be more intuitive for the readers who may be unfamiliar with data visualization. Something like a short explanation directly next to the visualization explaining what the reader should look for could make it easier to navigate. An improved version of this could allow readers to sort the different towns by the number of Airbnb listings, percentage of housing used as Airbnb rentals, or other means of measuring. A comparison chart could also make it easier to see the differences between communities without needing to explore the visualization. 

## Data Accuracy - 4/5
This story does many things well to establish the credibility of its data; *Seven Days* explains that it collected and analyzed 5,895 Vermont Airbnb listings and identifies the sources of other data, including the U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey and AirDNA. There are, however, som limitations to the data, including how Airbnb listings do not necessarily represent the number of unique opportunities or "hosts", since one person or company could operate multiple listings. The story also acknowledges how some listings belong to more traditional inns and bread and breakfasts, making it challenging when determining exactly how many listings represent housing the could otherwise be available to long-term residents. Another possible limitation is that the data is from 2017. The Airbnb market has changed significantly since then, so the findings would not necessarily describe Vermont's current short-term rentals.
Sources to check with include: 
+ Vermont's Department of Taxes
+ U.S. Census Bureau
+ Local town governments and zoning departments
+ Airbnb or other short-term rental platforms
+ AirDNA
+ Local housing organizations
The story generally does do a good job of explaining where they got their data from and acknowledges some of the limitations of measuring Airbnb's effect on housing. 

## Quality - 5/5
The story is executed at a high degree of quality; it combines data reporting with interviews and individual examples rather than relying just on statistic. For instance, the story introduces Stowe Airbnb host Josi Kytle, to show why someone may depend on short-term rentals for income. It then includes different perspectives from lodging businesses, town officials, and other residents. This adds meaning to the data because readers can see how the larger trend of Airbnb affects actual people and communities. 
A possible improvement could be to include more direct evidence connecting Airbnb growth to changes in housing prices or rental availability. The story addresses concerns about this relationship, but it also acknowledges that there are not always clear statistics proving Airbnb is responsible for those changes. 

## Type and Color
### Type - 4/5
The typography is clean and consistent with the rest of the *Seven Days* website; the headline is prominent and immediately introduces the subject of the story. The story also uses paragraphs and a metrics to break up the large amount of text. I think that their biggest strength is readability, because the design does not make the story feel overly complicated, despite containing a significant amount of information. 

### Color - 4/5
The colors work well with the overall design and do not distract from the reporting of the story. The visualization also uses shades of blue and red to distinguish different pieces of information and make it easier for the reader to understand. A possible improvement would be to make sure the visualization has contrast between categories and remains understandable for readers with color-vision deficiencies. In addition to color, maybe using labels would make the graphic more accessible or understandable. 

## Creativity - 5/5
The story uses creative elements effectively, particularly the interactive data visualization and combination of data with personal stories. The interactive feature allows readers to explore the Vermont communities with the most Airbnb rentals, and can get even more information by hovering over the graphic. The overall reporting gives context for why those numbers matter in the larger picture. The story also uses specific examples, like the amount hosts can earn and the percentage of housing stock represented by Airbnb listings, to make the larger trend more understandable. 
I think one of the stronger creative choices is that the story does not present Airbnb as simply good or bad, but instead, it shows the conflict between the economic benefits of short-term rentals and their potential effects on housing. That makes the data more interesting because there is a genuine question for readers to consider and think about.
